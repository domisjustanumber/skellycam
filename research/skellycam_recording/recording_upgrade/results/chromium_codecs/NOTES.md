## 1. Method

### 1.1 Test clips (fixtures)

- code: `scripts/chromium_codecs/make_clips.py:1-23`. Source frames: 60 consecutive real camera JPEGs (`corpus/c930e_720p_flash`, 1280x720), decoded with OpenCV; a 12-block binary pattern is stamped into the top 40 px band of each frame (block 0 white reference, block 1 black reference, blocks 2-11 = frame index, 10 bits). The stamped frames are saved as one raw bgr24 master.
- code: `make_clips.py:54-79`. 23 codec variants are encoded once each from the master into Matroska: AV1 (SVT-AV1 Main 8/10-bit 4:2:0, all-intra; libaom High 4:4:4 and Professional 4:2:2), VP9 profiles 0/1/2/3 incl. 4:2:2, lossless 4:4:4 and 4:2:0, all-intra, VP8, H.264 High 4:2:0 and High 4:2:2 (reference only, G7), HEVC Main/Main 10 (reference only), MJPEG 4:2:2, FFV1 v3 4:2:2, rawvideo yuv420p and yuyv422, Theora. GOP 30 except the `_intra` variants (GOP 1).
- code: `make_clips.py:81-89`, `:189-200`. Each master is remuxed with `-c copy` into mp4 (faststart), fragmented mp4 (`frag_keyframe+empty_moov+default_base_moof`), webm, mkv, mov, avi and ogg, so all containers of a codec carry the identical bitstream. Pairs FFmpeg refuses to mux are `n/m` (errors in `make_clips_log.json`, e.g. "vp9 only supported in MP4" for mov).
- code: `make_clips.py:204-207`. Elementary streams (IVF for AV1/VP9/VP8, Annex B for H.264/HEVC) are written for the WebCodecs test.
- code: `scripts/chromium_codecs/make_extras.py:1-14`. Camera-native MJPEG: the unmodified corpus JPEG bytes (frames 0-59) muxed with `-c copy` into mkv/avi/mov/mp4 (no index pattern, so only "does it open"). Still images: all real camera JPEGs of five corpus sets plus frame 0 of the flash set, and stamped frame 0 as PNG, WebP, AVIF 4:2:0/4:4:4 and JPEG XL.
- code: `scripts/chromium_codecs/make_big_clips.py:1-10`. Two ~120 MB VP9-lossless clips (600 real frames) in webm, mp4 with moov first and mp4 with moov at the end, for the serving test (small clips can be buffered whole, which hides missing Range support).
- **Deviation from the study rule:** the clips above were encoded with the ffmpeg 9.0.2 CLI (gyan build), and OpenCV from the skellycam venv (read-only use). The browser is what is under test here, but to cover the rule this session added `scripts/chromium_codecs/make_pyav19_clips.py`, which encodes and muxes the same stamped master with **PyAV 19.0.2** (`uv run --script`, PEP 723 pin `av==19.0.2`) for the royalty-free candidates: SVT-AV1 Main 8-bit 4:2:0 (GOP 30 and all-intra) in mp4/webm/mkv, VP9 profile 0 in mp4/webm/mkv, VP9 profile 0 all-intra in webm, VP9 lossless 4:4:4 in webm/mp4. measured: all 12 encoded and muxed (`pyav19_clips_log.json`); ffprobe 9.0.2 shows 2 keyframes in 60 packets for GOP 30, 60/60 for the intra clips, colour tags bt709/tv (inspection only, output in this session's log).

### 1.2 Pages (what each probe does)

All pages are in `scripts/chromium_codecs/harness/`. Each page posts one JSON result (to the local server, or over IPC in Electron).

| Page | What it does | Code |
|---|---|---|
| `probe.html` | `HTMLMediaElement.canPlayType`, `MediaSource.isTypeSupported`, `navigator.mediaCapabilities.decodingInfo` (type `file` and `media-source`, 1280x720 30 fps 5 Mb/s) for every codec string x container MIME; `VideoDecoder.isConfigSupported` with `prefer-hardware`/`prefer-software`/`no-preference`; `AudioDecoder.isConfigSupported`; `ImageDecoder.isTypeSupported`; feature presence (rVFC, MSE, WebCodecs, ImageDecoder, WebGPU...) | `probe.js:1-130` |
| `play.html` (mode src) | `<video src>`: waits for `loadeddata`/`error` (10 s); plays ~1.2 s and, in every `requestVideoFrameCallback`, draws the frame to a canvas, reads the index pattern and compares it to `round(mediaTime*30)`; then 10 paused seeks to the middle of frames 0, 7, 29, 30, 31, 45, 59, 12, 3, 58 (15 targets incl. 150/299/451/598 on big clips), reading the index after `seeked` + next rVFC | `play.js:1-115`, `common.js:24-38` |
| `play.html` (mode mse) | Same checks, but the whole file is fetched and appended to one `SourceBuffer` created with the clip's full MIME + codec string | `play.js:27-44`, `tasks.js:8-17` |
| `wc.html` | WebCodecs `VideoDecoder` on the elementary stream (IVF or Annex B split into access units), `prefer-hardware` and `prefer-software`; every output `VideoFrame` is drawn and its index compared with its output position | `wc.js:1-55`, `es_parse.js` |
| `img.html` | Every still image through `ImageDecoder`, `createImageBitmap` and `<img>`; checks the decoded content against a 16x9 grid of luma cell means (OpenCV decode for camera JPEGs, source pixels for the others) | `img.js:1-40` |

- code: `common.js:44-56`. Content check: mean absolute difference (MAD) between a 32x18 grid of 40x40-block luma means of the decoded frame and the same grid of the stamped source, in 8-bit luma levels. Lossy codecs and YUV conversion give non-zero MAD; it detects wrong frames, garbage and gross colour errors, not fine quality.
- code: `tasks.js:89-111`. Decoder identity: the runners subscribe to the DevTools `Media` domain and record `kVideoDecoderName` and `kIsPlatformVideoDecoder` per player. "Hardware decode" below means `kIsPlatformVideoDecoder = true` (decoder `D3D11VideoDecoder`).

## 8. Serving: file://, custom protocol (`protocol.handle`) and Range

measured: `raw/electron35_serving.json`, `raw/electron45_serving.json`, `raw/chrome156_serving.json` (12:01-12:19, UHD 770 absent; decoders as in section 3). 7 small clips (AV1 mp4/fmp4/intra, VP9 webm/mkv/lossless, H.264 mp4) with 10 seek targets and 3 big 120 MB VP9-lossless clips with 15 targets.

| mode (code: `electron_main.js:25-31,57-82`) | how media is served | E35 | E45 | Chrome 156 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| http-range | local HTTP, 206 + `Accept-Ranges` | all exact (10/10, big 15/15 incl. moov-at-end) | same | same |
| http-norange | local HTTP, always 200 full body, no `Accept-Ranges` | every seek lands on frame 0 (1/10, 1/15); `seekable` = [0,0] | same | same |
| file | page and media from `file://` | all exact | all exact | plays and seeks complete, but frame readback is blocked (canvas tainted: file:// media is cross-origin in Chrome), so frame accuracy NOT VERIFIED |
| app-range | `protocol.handle`, handler parses `Range` and returns 206 with `Content-Range` | all exact | all exact | n/a |
| app-range-nostream | same, scheme registered without the `stream` privilege | all exact | all exact | n/a |
| app-full | handler always returns 200 + full body + `Content-Length`, no `Accept-Ranges` | small clips exact; big 2/15; big mp4 with moov at end fails to open | same | n/a |
| app-netfetch | handler returns `net.fetch(file://...)` | 1/10 and 1/15 everywhere (`kIsStreaming=true`) | small clips mixed (some 10/10, some 1/10), big 1/15 | n/a |
| app-netfetch-headers | `net.fetch(file://..., {headers: req.headers})` (forwards `Range`) | 1/10, 1/15 | mixed small, big 1/15 | n/a |

- measured: the protocol request logs show every media request carried a `Range` header; the Range-parsing handlers answered 206 and the `net.fetch(file://)` handlers answered 200 even when the `Range` header was forwarded (E35: appnf 19/19 requests with Range, 0 x 206; appnfh 16/16, 0 x 206; E45 21/21 and 18/18, 0 x 206) (`protocolLog` in the serving JSONs).
- measured: Chromium reported `kIsStreaming=false` with `seekable` = whole duration for every exact case, and `kIsStreaming=true` with `seekable` = [0,0] for every case that seeked to frame 0 (http-norange, and each net.fetch case that failed; the E45 net.fetch cases that passed reported `kIsStreaming=false`) (`tables.md` serving section). inferred: whether a net.fetch-served clip is seekable depends on how the response happens to be classified per load, so it is not a usable design.
- So for G8/G9 in Electron: serve recordings either from `file://` or from a `protocol.handle` scheme whose handler implements Range itself (206 + `Content-Range`). Passing `net.fetch(file://)` through, or returning the full body, breaks seeking for files that are not fully buffered.

## 11. Limits and gaps

- Single machine, single GPU vendor active (NVIDIA Ampere). Hardware decode on Intel (QSV/D3D11 on the UHD 770 or Arc), AMD, Apple and Linux (VA-API) is NOT VERIFIED; the software paths (dav1d, libvpx) are what a machine without suitable hardware would use, and their cost is not measured (timing rule).
- 1280x720, 60 frames (600 for the big clips) only; 1080p/4K, long files, odd sizes and high frame rates were not played.
- Playback check covers ~1.2 s per clip at normal speed; reverse play, `playbackRate` changes, and many simultaneous `<video>` elements are not covered here (multi-element and seek timing are bench items for 23:00; G9 sync is the `chromium_sync_scrub` topic).
- H.264/HEVC were only made with libx264/libx265 defaults (B-frames, x265 open GOP). The fmp4 offset and HEVC mp4 seek errors were not root-caused and were not re-tested with B-frames off or closed GOP, or with NVENC output.
- The E35 H.264 WebCodecs key-frame failure is not root-caused.
- No crash-truncated files were played here (that is `crash_resilience`).
- Chrome `file://` frame accuracy not verified (canvas taint); Chrome custom protocols not applicable.
- Fetching single JPEG frames out of an mkv/mp4 by byte range (for G1 + G9 together) was not tested.
- AV1 4:4:4/4:2:2 and VP9 profile 1-3 clips came from the ffmpeg CLI (libaom/libvpx), not PyAV 19; PyAV 19 coverage is limited to section 9.
- The serving runs (12:01-12:19) predate the UHD 770 coming back; they were not rerun (decoder names there match section 3).
- No audio files were played (audio rows are API probes only).
