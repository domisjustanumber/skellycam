# Overnight unattended run, 2026-10-27 — Report

Status as of 2026-10-28: **finished**. Both workflows completed with 0 errors. This folder holds the as-run plan, log and helper scripts. The results are in [`../usb_cameras/`](../usb_cameras/) and [`../frame_ring_buffer/`](../frame_ring_buffer/).

All times are local (EDT, UTC−4) unless marked Z (UTC). The machine is "Gerald": Intel Core i7-14700K (20 cores, 28 logical), 64 GB DDR5 running at 4000 MT/s (rated 6400), Windows 11 Home 10.0.26300, power plan Balanced ([baseline_result.json](../frame_ring_buffer/study_state/baseline_result.json), `environment`). The camera run ran without admin rights ([RERUN_20261027.md](../usb_cameras/results/windows/Gerald/RERUN_20261027.md)).

## Executive summary

On the evening of 2026-10-27, Dom asked for two unattended jobs to start once the machine was idle after 23:00. The first re-ran every USB camera test that needs no admin rights, replug or reboot. The second re-ran the frame ring buffer benchmarks and finished that study ([PLAN.md](PLAN.md)). An idle watcher script started the camera re-run (`wf_ef322d5c-d99`) at 23:03. It finished at 00:57 with 13 of 13 agents and 0 errors. The ring buffer workflow (`wf_8fd1ec97-b0e`) then ran from 00:57 to 07:32 with 10 of 10 agents and 0 errors ([OVERNIGHT_LOG.md](OVERNIGHT_LOG.md)). In-scope camera devnodes stayed healthy at every check. The only failures were the expected bandwidth refusals on the shared USB hub ([RERUN_20261027.md](../usb_cameras/results/windows/Gerald/RERUN_20261027.md)).

The run was less controlled than intended in three ways:
- **The idle gate only applied at the start.** The watcher fired on its first check, at 23:02:07, because nobody had touched the machine for 8,697 s (about 2.4 h). After that, up to six camera agents shared the machine and the camera lock, and they changed camera settings under each other.
- **The "excluded" Razer Kiyo Pro was still contacted.** The camera run touched it through enumeration paths. The ring buffer run's real-camera prototypes probably streamed it by index (P9) and tried to open it by name (P9 and P9b, failing every time), because the exclusion was never passed to that workflow.
- **The generic camera re-arrived twice** with no cause recorded.

The ring buffer stages had their own per-benchmark load rule, and every measuring step met it except one row hit by a Defender scan. Those are the cleanest performance numbers this project has so far.

For skellycam, the lessons are about running long unattended jobs:
- Enforce hardware exclusions in every workflow, or physically unplug the device.
- Isolate or reset camera controls for each capture.
- Re-check camera health after every stage that touches cameras.

For the findings themselves, see the [USB camera report](../usb_cameras/REPORT.md) and the [frame ring buffer report](../frame_ring_buffer/REPORT.md).

## Key findings

1. **Both workflows completed without errors (measured).** The camera re-run `wf_ef322d5c-d99` ran 23:03:10–00:57:38 (1 h 54 min) with 13 of 13 agents: six area re-runs, six adversarial verifiers and one consolidation. The ring buffer run `wf_8fd1ec97-b0e` ran 00:57:41–07:32:48 (6 h 35 min) with 10 of 10 agents ([OVERNIGHT_LOG.md](OVERNIGHT_LOG.md); [overnight_rerun_workflow.js](../usb_cameras/workflows/overnight_rerun_workflow.js)). A third workflow, `wf_264799ca-33d`, later filled three summary Claude Docs ([README.md](README.md); [script](workflows/summary-reports-three-docs-wf_264799ca-33d.js)). No local log, journal or result records when it ran or how it ended.

2. **The idle gate did not test idleness during the night (measured, plus inference).** The watcher armed at 23:02:06 and reported IDLE on its first check at 23:02:07: input idle 8,697 s, CPU 3.3% ([OVERNIGHT_LOG.md](OVERNIGHT_LOG.md)). Its rule was no input for 15 min and mean CPU below 25% over three samples taken 5 s apart. It polls every 60 s, exits on the first check that passes, and does not watch the machine after that ([idle_watch.ps1](idle_watch.ps1)). In practice, the 23:00 start time was the only real gate (inferred).

3. **In-scope cameras stayed healthy through the camera run (measured).** The C922 (046D:085C), C930e (046D:0843) and generic camera (4C4A:4A55) read DevNodeStatus 0x0180200A at every check. Their composite parents read 0x0180600A, with ProblemCode 0. DN_NEED_RESTART was never set. The consolidation agent's last check was at 04:53Z, and the session log records all camera devnodes OK at 00:57:38 local (04:57Z) ([OVERNIGHT_LOG.md](OVERNIGHT_LOG.md)). No device was restarted. There was no MF_E_REBOOT_REQUIRED or 0x8007001F ([RERUN_20261027.md](../usb_cameras/results/windows/Gerald/RERUN_20261027.md#camera-health-through-the-night)). The only failures were the expected shared-hub refusals: MF 0x800718CF and DirectShow OSError 5 when the C930e and the generic camera streamed together. There were also two transient MF `DeviceNotFound` misses of the generic camera right after such a refusal. No devnode check is recorded after the ring buffer run used the cameras at about 02:55–03:19 local (finding 6).

4. **The generic camera re-arrived twice without explanation (measured).** Its LastArrivalDate moved to 03:18:48Z and then to 04:12:37Z, both while capture agents were running. Neither the MJPEG nor the bandwidth agent reported a disconnect, and no Kernel-PnP configuration event was logged. It came back healthy with only its USB address changed (11→15). The other cameras kept their 18:39:5xZ arrival times ([RERUN_20261027.md](../usb_cameras/results/windows/Gerald/RERUN_20261027.md#camera-health-through-the-night)).

5. **The camera run touched the excluded Kiyo Pro without streaming it (measured, from source code and logs).** libusb and rawusb enumeration send hub IOCTLs (driver control requests), including a real GET_DESCRIPTOR(config), to every device before any exclude filter applies. GStreamer `ksvideosrc` opens a handle on every KS (kernel streaming) capture filter before it matches the device name, and it did this at 03:16Z, 03:27Z and 03:37Z. The verifiers marked the agents' own "never touched" claims **weakened** (libusb) and **refuted** (ksvideosrc). The Kiyo's devnode stayed healthy ([RERUN_20261027.md](../usb_cameras/results/windows/Gerald/RERUN_20261027.md#kiyo-contact); [OVERNIGHT_LOG.md](OVERNIGHT_LOG.md)).

6. **The ring buffer run opened the excluded Kiyo outright (measured; one detail inferred).** The prototypes stage ran P9 (real cameras) at 02:55–03:17 local (a first attempt with the wrong property order at 02:55–03:09, then the kept run at 03:10–03:17) and P9b at 03:18–03:19 (`load_p9_real_camera.json`, `run1_fourcc_first_load_p9_real_camera.json`, `load_p9b_pyav_dshow_probe.json` in [prototypes/results/](../frame_ring_buffer/prototypes/results/)). P9 opened DirectShow indices 0–3 one at a time and streamed every index at 23–30 fps ([p9_real_camera.md](../frame_ring_buffer/prototypes/results/p9_real_camera.md)). Its README says the four attached cameras were the c922, C930e, Razer Kiyo Pro and "USB Camera" ([prototypes/README.md](../frame_ring_buffer/prototypes/README.md)). So one index was probably the Kiyo (inferred). P9 did not record its index-to-camera mapping. Earlier that night, though, the naming verifier mapped DirectShow index 0 to the C922, 1 to the generic camera and 2 to the C930e, and the Kiyo moved to index 3. P9's index 1 ran at the generic camera's 25.7–25.8 fps ([rerun_20261027_reports_and_verifications.json](../usb_cameras/results/agent_reports/rerun_20261027_reports_and_verifications.json), naming verification; [RERUN_20261027.md](../usb_cameras/results/windows/Gerald/RERUN_20261027.md#goal-4-naming)). P9's own PyAV check also tried "Razer Kiyo Pro" by name at 720p60 and 1080p60, and both attempts failed with OSError 5. P9b tried to open "Razer Kiyo Pro" by name in five modes, and every attempt failed with OSError 5 ([p9b_pyav_dshow_probe.md](../frame_ring_buffer/prototypes/results/p9b_pyav_dshow_probe.md)). The ring buffer workflow scripts never mention the Kiyo ([overnight_workflow.js](../frame_ring_buffer/study_state/overnight_workflow.js)), so the exclusion lived only in the camera workflow's prompts. The overnight log and the camera re-run report do not mention this contact.

7. **The camera run was not quiet (measured).** Six re-run agents and then six verifiers shared the machine as a per-area pipeline, so up to six agents ran at once. Whole-machine CPU was mostly 2–21%, with brief peaks of 57–60%. Waits for camlock, the camera lock, reached 472 s. Agents changed camera controls under each other: exposure flipped from auto to manual between 03:11Z and 03:35Z, and timing's "auto exposure" capture actually ran at the C930e's leftover manual −5 ([RERUN_20261027.md](../usb_cameras/results/windows/Gerald/RERUN_20261027.md#conditions)). The agents left the cameras at manual exposure −7 (C930e reads back −5), white balance 4500 K and AE priority 0, and nothing reverted them ([PROGRESS.md](../usb_cameras/PROGRESS.md)).

8. **LogiTune could not be kept closed (measured).** LogiTuneAgent respawned within about 10 s when stopped at 23:02 ([OVERNIGHT_LOG.md](OVERNIGHT_LOG.md)). Agents closed it again at 03:07Z, 03:39Z and 03:48Z, and `LogiTuneUpdaterService` brought it back within seconds each time. Stopping the service was not approved, so no LogiTune-free C930e test was possible ([RERUN_20261027.md](../usb_cameras/results/windows/Gerald/RERUN_20261027.md#camera-health-through-the-night)).

9. **Every ring buffer measuring step met a stricter, per-run idle rule, with one exception and one disclosed caveat (measured).** The rule: CPU from processes outside the benchmark ("background") below 10% in both mean and p95, and system CPU below 10% in 3 s windows before and after each run. It was recorded by `run_with_load.py` for every step. Background means were 2.0–4.1% for the baseline (00:59–01:27), 1.6–3.9% for the prototypes (01:44–03:20) and 1.6–3.7% for the C++ comparison (03:44–04:44). The revision stage (06:10–07:06) had background means of 1.9–4.6% ([PROVISIONAL.md](../frame_ring_buffer/PROVISIONAL.md); [revision/README.md](../frame_ring_buffer/revision/README.md) said 2.4–4.6% until it was corrected on 2026-10-28). See also [prototypes load_summary.md](../frame_ring_buffer/prototypes/results/load_summary.md) and [cpp/README.md](../frame_ring_buffer/cpp/README.md). The exception and the caveat:
   - `p10_msmf_threads` was marked provisional because a Defender scan peaked at 22.7% background, with 18.2% system CPU afterwards, mostly MsMpEng. It was superseded by the idle `p10v` run.

10. **Running on a quiet machine moved the headline benchmark, but not the verdict (measured, synthetic cameras).** For five 1080p MJPEG cameras through the real SkellyCam stack with the UI attached, end-to-end rate per camera rose from 44.01/46.78 fps on the loaded 2026-10-25 run to 53.56/53.57 fps idle. ## Techniques compared

Three ways of running the benchmarks were used: a loaded daytime run, a gated but concurrent overnight run, and a sequential overnight run that recorded load per benchmark. The table compares them on how far their numbers can be trusted.

| Run design | Load control | Concurrency | Recorded load | Device/control isolation | What its numbers are good for | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ring buffer, 2026-10-25 daytime ([PROVISIONAL.md](../frame_ring_buffer/PROVISIONAL.md)) | None; browser, IDE, chat apps and other agents active | Shared with normal use | One snapshot: 62% CPU, 61% memory | n/a (synthetic cameras) | Relative comparisons within one script | **Provisional**; absolute numbers superseded (e.g. 44–47 vs 53.6 fps idle) |
| Camera re-run `wf_ef322d5c-d99`, overnight ([RERUN_20261027.md](../usb_cameras/results/windows/Gerald/RERUN_20261027.md)) | Idle watcher once at start (15 min no input, CPU < 25%) | Up to 6 agents at once; camlock serialises capture (waits up to 472 s) | Machine CPU at start/end of CPU-sensitive runs: mostly 2–21%, peaks 57–60% | Weak: controls changed under each other; Kiyo contacted by enumeration and ksvideosrc | Function, fps, bandwidth admission, native-JPEG checks | **Good for function and fps; weak for CPU% and control-dependent results** (e.g. MJPEG process CPU 1.3–2.3x the 10-25 values, unexplained) |
| Ring buffer `wf_8fd1ec97-b0e`, overnight ([PROVISIONAL.md](../frame_ring_buffer/PROVISIONAL.md)) | Per-benchmark idle rule (background mean and p95 < 10%, system < 10% before/after) | Stages run one at a time | Every step in `load_*.json`; background mean 1.6–4.6% | Exclusion not passed on; P9/P9b opened the Kiyo | Absolute throughput, latency and CPU | **Best design**; 130/136 throughput rows reproduced within ±15% |

The per-benchmark load record is the method to copy. It turns "the machine was probably quiet" into a label on every result, and it caught the Defender scan. Its caveat is that it measures CPU only. It did not record CPU frequency or power state, which the camera run left as one possible cause of the unexplained MJPEG CPU change. It also did not stop the run from opening hardware it should have skipped.

## Areas of interest

- **An exclusion in one workflow does not carry over to the next.** The Kiyo exclusion was written into the camera workflow's prompts, and the ring buffer workflow, run straight after on the same machine, opened the camera anyway (findings 5–6). Any shared-machine test plan needs one exclusion list that every stage reads, or a physical unplug.
- **The Kiyo would not open through FFmpeg DirectShow in any mode** (OSError 5) during P9b. The cause was not established ([prototypes/README.md](../frame_ring_buffer/prototypes/README.md)). Nothing shows whether this relates to the earlier enumeration contact.
- **The P9 explanation for under-30 fps may be wrong (inferred).** P9 blamed "exposure in a dark room and camera mode limits". The camera run had left all cameras at manual exposure −7 with AE priority 0, which should stop exposure from lowering fps ([PROGRESS.md](../usb_cameras/PROGRESS.md); [RERUN_20261027.md](../usb_cameras/results/windows/Gerald/RERUN_20261027.md#conditions)). P9b also shows that 60 fps requests fail or fall back to 30 on these cameras. So mode limits are the more likely cause. P9 did not record its exposure settings.
- **Display keep-awake mostly worked.** [keep_display_awake.ps1](keep_display_awake.ps1) set `ES_CONTINUOUS | ES_SYSTEM_REQUIRED | ES_DISPLAY_REQUIRED` and nudged the mouse 1 px to wake the screen. It was released at 00:57. One of 10 timing flash runs (run d) had DXGI refresh glitches and was marked invalid. The C922 luma fell to about 4 late in the MJPEG run, "probably when the monitor dimmed" (hypothesis in [RERUN_20261027.md](../usb_cameras/results/windows/Gerald/RERUN_20261027.md#conditions)). Nothing held the system awake during the 6.5 h ring buffer run. It completed anyway, but the sleep settings were not recorded.
- **Two unattributed reboots happened on 10-27, before the run.** One was a power-off initiated by winlogon on behalf of SYSTEM at 00:01:31Z, with boot at 14:26:08Z. The other was an unexpected shutdown at 13:46:22 local (17:46Z), with boot at 18:39:40Z ([boot_events_since_20261025.json](../usb_cameras/results/windows/Gerald/naming_rerun_20261027/boot_events_since_20261025.json)). They gave opportunistic naming-stability evidence. For future long runs they are a reliability risk.
- **Harness hazards in unattended runs.** A smoke-test process blocked on a full stdout pipe for 12 minutes. A `run_concurrency` combo hung and was killed after about 94 s, because there is no per-combo timeout. An outer `timeout` counts the camlock wait. And re-running the documented ring buffer commands overwrites the cited results (152 files new or changed), so a snapshot/restore step and `FRAME_PIPELINE_RESULTS_DIR` were added ([RERUN_20261027.md](../usb_cameras/results/windows/Gerald/RERUN_20261027.md#harness-bugs-still-open); [revision/README.md](../frame_ring_buffer/revision/README.md); [reproducibility/README.md](../frame_ring_buffer/reproducibility/README.md)).

## Limitations and caveats

- **One machine, one night.** All numbers come from Gerald on 2026-10-27/28. Results ran at 4000 MT/s memory on a 6400-rated kit; how much that lowers memory-copy numbers was not tested.
- **The camera run's CPU% and exposure-sensitive numbers carry load and cross-agent interference.** Treat them as provisional until re-run sequentially.
- **Camera health was checked only up to 04:57Z (00:57 local).** The ring buffer run's camera use at about 06:55–07:19Z has no devnode check after it, and the nearest check before it was 2 h earlier.
- **Which DirectShow index was the Kiyo in P9 is not recorded.** The claim that P9 streamed it rests on the prototypes README's list of attached cameras. The by-name attempts on it in P9 and P9b are certain; the naming verifier's earlier DirectShow mapping (Kiyo at index 3) supports the index claim.
- **All 5×60 fps ring buffer numbers use synthetic cameras.**
- **Errata in other write-ups:** see finding 13 (stale RESUME.md lines, missing journals). The README index says `usb_cameras` goals 2 and 3 "need corrections" ([../README.md](../README.md)). The USB camera report's own errata list also covers goal 4: MF/DShow indices and naming listing time ([../usb_cameras/REPORT.md](../usb_cameras/REPORT.md)).
- **The Claude Docs summaries written by `wf_264799ca-33d` were not checked** against local files for this report.

## Proposed further investigation

1. **Make exclusions enforceable (highest).** Physically unplug excluded cameras for unattended runs, or give every workflow one shared exclusion list and block paths that touch every device (libusb/rawusb enumeration, ksvideosrc, index-based OpenCV opens). *Why:* the Kiyo was contacted by both workflows. *Decides:* whether software-only exclusion is ever safe on this machine.
2. **Isolate camera controls for each capture.** Either give each control-dependent test exclusive use of a camera, or set and read back exposure, white balance and AE priority immediately before and after every capture, then reset. *Why:* agents changed controls under each other. *Decides:* whether the C930e exposure, period-mode and C922 1080p@5 results are camera behaviour or test interference.
3. **Re-run the camera CPU-sensitive tests sequentially, with the ring buffer's load rule, plus the 27 missed tests.** *Why:* MJPEG process CPU was 1.3–2.3x the 10-25 values with no explanation, and the verifiers listed 27 skipped-but-runnable tests. *Decides:* whether the CPU change is real, and closes the gaps.
4. **Log the system state during unattended runs.** Record PnP/USB arrival events, devnode status after every camera-touching stage, CPU frequency and power state, Defender activity and sleep/display settings. *Why:* there were two unexplained generic-camera re-arrivals, two unattributed reboots on 10-27, an unexplained CPU shift and a Defender hit. *Decides:* the causes of these, before the next long run.
5. **Harden the harness for unattended use.** Add per-step timeouts that exclude lock wait, drain or redirect child stdout, and put a per-combo timeout in `run_concurrency.py`. *Why:* there was a 12 min blocked process and a 94 s hang. *Decides:* whether a failed step can stall or contaminate the next.
6. **Keep the record complete.** Save journals for every workflow (`wf_8fd1ec97-b0e`, `wf_264799ca-33d`). Fix the stale RESUME.md lines. Let agents write their summary files. *Why:* this report had to reconstruct state from logs. *Decides:* nothing new, but avoids repeating this archaeology.
